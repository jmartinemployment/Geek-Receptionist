# Connect Google Drive with one narrow scope

Geek-Receptionist can let each customer connect their own Google Drive using a single non-sensitive scope, `drive.file`, a server-side refresh token per tenant, and a Drive folder that the product itself creates. Google classifies `drive.file` as non-sensitive, which means no sensitive or restricted scope review and no security assessment, only the lighter "basic" verification and brand verification that govern how the consent screen looks ([Drive scopes guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/api-specific-auth); [Cloud Console help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463073)). Recordings of 1 to 100 MB should go up through resumable upload, with a pre-generated file id so a retried create cannot make a duplicate ([Manage uploads](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/manage-uploads)). The connection needs a visible proof step, because the two vendors confirmed in the research (Fireflies, Dialpad) document connect and disconnect but no test file, "connected as" line or last-upload time. The step also needs distinct failure states, because the connection can die in at least six different ways, each with a different fix. The biggest open risks are not in Google's documentation at all: what an app can still see after a customer revokes and reconnects, whether picking a folder lets the app write into it, and whether a non-sensitive-only production app still shows an "unverified app" screen. This report separates what the sources state (**Verified**) from what follows from them (**Inference**) and from what the research did not find (**Gaps**). It ends with the screens and states a connect step needs.

## One non-sensitive scope keeps verification light

**Verified.** `drive.file`, `drive.appdata` and `drive.install` are listed as non-sensitive. `drive` and `drive.readonly` are restricted, and a security assessment applies only when restricted-scope data is stored on servers ([Drive scopes guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/api-specific-auth)). The scope lets an app create files and modify files it created or that the user opened with it, including via the Google Picker. Google says the non-sensitive classification "allows for a more streamlined verification process" and recommends this scope for most uses (same source). An app using only non-sensitive scopes does not need full verification, but must complete brand verification to show its name and logo on the consent screen ([Cloud Console help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463073)). Production apps still need a public homepage on a verified domain linking terms of service and a privacy policy, consent-screen scopes that match the scopes requested, and separate Cloud projects for dev, staging and production ([OAuth policies](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/policies)).

The Testing status is a trap for a launch. A Testing-status app is capped at 100 listed test users and its authorizations, including offline refresh tokens, expire after 7 days ([Cloud Console help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)). A tenant connected under Testing status would silently lose its Drive connection a week later. In production, any Google account can authorize, but an app showing the unverified-app screen is limited to 100 new users over the project's lifetime, and that cap cannot be reset (same source).

**Inference.** Because the 100-user lifetime cap attaches to unverified apps, a multi-tenant product should complete brand verification before the first paying customer even though no scope review is required. If the same OAuth client also requests Calendar scopes, which Google gives as an example of a sensitive scope needing review ([Sensitive scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/sensitive-scope-verification)), the whole consent screen falls under sensitive-scope verification, which can take up to 10 days (same source). A separate OAuth client for Drive would let it stay entirely in the non-sensitive tier and would isolate token revocation, because a combined token revoked in one account revokes every scope it carries ([OAuth web-server guide](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/web-server)). This is a design judgment; Google gave no guidance on one client versus several.

**Gaps.** The exact brand-verification steps, timing and required fields were not retrieved (the help page was truncated and points to a Verification Requirements page that was not read). Whether a production app with only `drive.file` shows the unverified-app screen or counts toward the 100-user cap is not stated in the pages read. Treat both as questions to settle in the Google Cloud console before launch.

## Tokens are the fragile part of the connection

**Verified.** Request `access_type=offline` to receive a refresh token. It generally comes back only on the first authorization, so a lost token means repeating consent, and `prompt=consent` forces a new one ([OAuth web-server guide](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/web-server)). A refresh token stops working when the user revokes access, after six months unused, when the user's live-token cap is reached (100 per account per client ID, oldest invalidated silently), when an admin sets a requested service to Restricted (error `admin_policy_enforced`), when time-based access ends, and, for Testing apps, after 7 days ([OAuth 2.0 overview](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2)). The password-change rule applies to tokens carrying Gmail scopes. On refresh, `invalid_grant` typically means the token expired or was invalidated or the account was deleted or disabled, and the fix is re-authentication and new consent ([OAuth web-server guide](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/web-server)). Redirect URIs must be HTTPS (localhost exempt) and match a registered URI exactly, including scheme, case and trailing slash, with no wildcards or fragments (same source). Google's policies say to store tokens encrypted and to integrate Cross-Account Protection for revocation notices ([OAuth policies](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/policies)).

Workspace administrators add a second failure source. Admins set each app, by OAuth client ID, to Trusted, Specific Google data, Limited or Blocked, per organizational unit, and a change can take up to 24 hours; blocked users are told the app is blocked, with an optional custom message from the admin ([Workspace admin help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/apps/control-which-apps-access-google-workspace-data)). `drive.file` is not on the high-risk Drive scope list, so even when an admin restricts Drive, users can still be allowed to grant non-high-risk scopes (same source). Replit's troubleshooting page reflects what customers meet in practice: "This app is blocked" is typically a Workspace setting, the fix is for the admin to trust the client ID and wait 5 to 10 minutes, and a plain `@gmail.com` account cannot change the policy itself ([Replit docs](https://docs.replit.com/features/troubleshooting/debugging-google-connectors.md)).

**Inference.** `invalid_grant` and `admin_policy_enforced` should both mean "connection broken": mark the tenant's Drive connection disconnected, stop uploads, show the owner a reconnect prompt, and never drop recordings silently. Avoid `prompt=consent` on every login, because each use mints another token toward the 100-token cap. Give Workspace customers the client ID and scope list to forward to their admin. A single-scope request means the consent screen has no checkboxes, so approve and deny are the only outcomes; a partial grant should not occur ([Granular permissions](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/resources/granular-permissions)).

**Gaps.** Whether Google rotates refresh tokens on use is not stated. The exact wording of the "app blocked" and unverified-app screens was not captured. Pop-up, third-party-cookie and Safari tracking-prevention effects on a redirect flow versus a pop-up flow were not researched; only Pipedrive documents the cookie and pop-up advice ([Pipedrive help](https://intercom.help/pipedrive/en/articles/14529883-how-can-i-troubleshoot-and-resolve-google-drive-integration-issues-in-pipedrive)).

## Resumable uploads, with ids and properties set up front

**Verified.** Simple and multipart uploads stop at 5 MB; resumable upload is for larger files and "a good choice for most applications" at the cost of one extra request ([Manage uploads](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/manage-uploads)). A POST with `uploadType=resumable` returns a session URI in the `Location` header, which expires after one week; data goes up by PUT, in chunks that are multiples of 256 KB except the last. To resume, send an empty PUT with `Content-Range: bytes */<total>`: 200 or 201 means done, 308 means continue from the `Range` header, and 404 means the session expired and the upload restarts (same source). Retry on 5xx, interruptions and 403 rate limits; restart with a new session on other 4xx errors (same source). Maximum upload size is 5 TB ([Usage limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits)).

For idempotency, `files.generateIds` returns pre-generated ids. If the first create succeeded, a retry returns 409 Conflict and no duplicate appears ([generateIds](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/reference/rest/v3/files/generateIds); [Manage uploads](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/manage-uploads)). `appProperties` are private to the requesting app, searchable in `q`, and limited to 124 bytes per key plus value, 30 private per app ([Properties](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/properties)). `md5Checksum` and `size` come back for binary files and can verify an upload ([files resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/reference/rest/v3/files)). Errors split into retryable and not. 429 and 403 `rateLimitExceeded` or `userRateLimitExceeded` take exponential backoff, `min((2^n) + random_ms, max_backoff)`, with a typical maximum of 32 or 64 seconds. 403 `storageQuotaExceeded` means the user's Drive is full and needs the user to act ([Usage limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits); [Handle errors](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/handle-errors)). Quotas are generous: `files.create` costs 50 units against 325,000 per user per minute, and a user can upload 750 GB per day ([Usage limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits)). Service accounts have no storage and cannot own files, so the customer's own OAuth user must own the recordings.

**Inference.** Use resumable for audio and, for one code path, for transcripts and summaries too. Persist the session URI and the pre-generated file id when the call record is created, tag each file with `appProperties` (call id, tenant id, kind), and after a 409 check `size` and `md5Checksum` before treating it as success. Retry the rate and server errors; do not retry `storageQuotaExceeded`, surface it as "Drive full". Treat `notFound` on the stored folder id as folder gone or access lost and report it rather than silently recreating the folder, which matches the project's fail-loud rule. Folder item limits (500,000 per folder, 100 nesting levels) are not a practical constraint at small-business call volumes ([Folders](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/folder)).

**Gaps.** The documentation does not say whether the 409 behavior applies at resumable-session creation specifically, nor what Google sends when a Workspace admin storage cap is hit. No source documents what the app sees if the customer deletes or moves the folder; that the id survives a move is inferred from the API model.

## Let the product make the folder, then prove it works

**Verified.** Both confirmed vendors create their own folders. Fireflies writes to "Fireflies Transcripts", "Fireflies Summaries" and "Fireflies Recordings", after a Connect click on an Integrations page and Google sign-in, and the article describes no folder choice. It then shows toggles for transcript, recording and summary, a Save button that activates the sync, an optional "Push past meetings" with a date range, admin Team Permissions, and Disconnect ([Fireflies guide](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/3132328743-how-to-integrate-fireflies-with-google-drive)). Dialpad has the user select "Connect Google Drive", choose a Google Workspace account and Continue, and then turn on "Save Recordings to Google Drive" in a separate setting; per a search summary of Dialpad's page (the page returned 403 to direct fetch), it operates only in "Dialpad Recordings" and "Dialpad Voicemails" and does not delete files from the customer's Drive ([Dialpad Record a Call](https://help.dialpad.com/docs/record-a-call); [Dialpad Google Workspace](https://help.dialpad.com/docs/sync-and-integrate-with-google-workspace)). Fireflies' security guide states "You retain full ownership of your recordings and transcripts," that it does not train AI on meeting data, and that audio, transcript and summary each get their own retention period ([Fireflies security guide](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/7434774675-responsible-and-secure-meeting-notetaking-with-fireflies)).

Neither confirmed vendor page documents a test file, "connected as" line, folder link, last-upload time or any deleted-folder handling. The clearest reconnect pattern found is Pipedrive's: the upload error reads "Could not connect to Google Drive. Reconnect and try again", and the fix is to disconnect, remove the app's permissions in the Google Account, allow cookies and pop-ups, then add the account again and enable all permissions ([Pipedrive help](https://intercom.help/pipedrive/en/articles/14529883-how-can-i-troubleshoot-and-resolve-google-drive-integration-issues-in-pipedrive)). Teamwork and Formstack describe the same disable-and-re-enable or log-in-again fix for 401 and `invalid_grant`, per search summaries ([Teamwork](https://support.teamwork.com/projects/addons-and-integrations/reconnecting-your-google-drive-integration); [Formstack](https://help.formstack.com/hc/en-us/articles/44593159533843)). The Google Picker is the documented route for handing an app an existing file or folder under `drive.file`; it needs an API key, the Cloud project number as the app id, and a browser-side token, and `setSelectFolderEnabled` allows folder selection ([Web picker](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/picker/guides/web-picker); [Reference](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/picker/reference/picker.docsview.setselectfolderenabled)).

**Inference.** A vendor-created folder fits `drive.file` best: the app owns what it creates and needs no Picker, no API key and no browser-side token. A connection test can be built from calls that `drive.file` permits: `files.get` the folder id for `trashed` and `capabilities.canAddChildren`, create a 1-byte file tagged `purpose: connectionTest`, read it back, then `files.delete` it, which bypasses trash and leaves no clutter ([files.delete](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/reference/rest/v3/files/delete)). If the delete returns 403, show it rather than ignoring it. A "connected as" line, a folder link and a last-upload time would let the screen tell the failure classes apart; no vendor page shows this, so it is original design, not copied convention. The consent screen should say what the customer is approving and what the product will never do: per Google's description it covers only files "you use with this app", and the product will not read the rest of the Drive.

**Gaps.** What an app can see after the customer revokes and reconnects is the largest unknown. Google's help page says only that the app can no longer access the account; a 2015 Stack Overflow report describes an app losing sight of its own files after disconnect, while draw.io says its files stay in the user's Drive ([draw.io](https://drawio.com/doc/faq/google-drive-revoke-access)). Plan to test it before promising customers that old recordings stay linked. The consent-screen sentence "See, edit, create, and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app" came from a search snippet and was not seen on a fetched page. Whether picking a folder grants create-in-folder, whether `drive.file` reaches an existing shared-drive folder, and whether `files.list` is limited to app-authorized files rest on community posts or are undocumented. Shared drives need `supportsAllDrives=true` and have different ownership rules, with no role requirements stated in the pages read. `about.get` storage quota under `drive.file` was not checked. Gong, Loom, Calendly, RingCentral, Twilio, native Zoom and Otter had no first-party Drive documentation retrievable, so claims about "what other products do" rest on Fireflies, Dialpad, Pipedrive, Teamwork and Formstack.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the question. Choosing a scope is the easy part; `drive.file` is already the low-friction answer. The real work is the lifecycle after the first click: a token that can die from six causes, a Workspace admin who can block the app without the owner knowing why, and a Drive that can fill or lose its folder. Competitors' public help pages barely cover this, so the failure-state design here has no convention to copy and needs its own testing.

Three things should be settled before building: a revoke-and-reconnect test of whether files the app created remain visible, a check in the Cloud console of whether a `drive.file`-only production app shows the unverified screen, and a decision on whether Drive gets its own OAuth client so it never inherits Calendar's sensitive-scope review. Whether the product keeps any copy of a recording is outside this research and is governed by the project's zero-data-retention rule.

## What this means for the signup screens

1. Explain screen, before leaving for Google. Say what is sent to Drive (recordings, transcripts, summaries, with the choices shown as separate toggles as Fireflies does), that the files are the customer's own and stay in their Drive, that the product only touches the folder it creates, and that the customer can disconnect at any time. Tied to: narrow `drive.file` scope and the Fireflies and Dialpad ownership statements.
2. Which-account guidance on the same screen. Tell the owner to sign in with the Google account whose Drive should hold the recordings, and say that a Google Workspace account may need its administrator's approval. Tied to: admin controls and the Replit and Pipedrive wrong-account findings.
3. Google consent hand-off state. Show a short "waiting for Google" state on return that finishes the connection on the server (the code exchange, the stored encrypted refresh token, the `state` check). Tied to: redirect URI rules and the offline token behavior.
4. Denied state. If the owner declines or closes the consent screen, show that nothing was connected and offer to try again. A single scope gives approve or deny only. Tied to: granular-permissions finding.
5. Blocked-by-administrator state. Tell the owner the Workspace admin has blocked the app, give the app name and OAuth client ID to forward, say the change can take up to 24 hours, and offer connecting a different Google account. Tied to: Workspace admin controls.
6. Unverified-app or browser-trouble guidance state. Brief help for the unverified-app screen, blocked pop-ups, third-party cookies and a retry in a private window. Tied to: the 100-user cap and warning behavior, the Pipedrive and Replit advice. The exact Google wording is a gap to capture first.
7. Creating-and-testing state. Show progress while the product creates its folder and runs the test (check the folder, write a small file, read it back, delete it). Tied to: connection test sequence.
8. Connected state. Show the connected Google account email, a link to the folder, the result and time of the last test, and Disconnect and Test again actions. Tied to: absence of any such display among vendors, and the test sequence.
9. Test-failed state, with a reason for each class: Drive full (`storageQuotaExceeded`, owner frees space), folder missing or trashed (`notFound`, `trashed`), no write permission, and rate or server trouble (retried automatically). Nothing is recreated or hidden silently. Tied to: errors and retry findings.
10. Connection-lost state, shown on the dashboard and on any later sign-in. Cover revoked access, `invalid_grant`, six months unused, and `admin_policy_enforced`, with a Reconnect button and a statement that recordings that could not be delivered are named, not dropped. Tied to: refresh-token death causes and Pipedrive's reconnect pattern.
11. Disconnect confirmation. Say that existing files stay in the customer's Drive, that uploads stop, and that reconnecting may not restore the app's view of older files until tested. Tied to: the unresolved revocation gap and draw.io's statement.
