# Does any law make a private business speak another language?

Companion to `multilingual-receptionist.md`. That file asked what it would take to *add* languages. This one asks the
prior question: whether anything **requires** it, because a legal duty and a marketing choice are different settings on
the form and the answer changes who decides.

**The short answer, and it is the useful one: no state law found requires a private business to provide SPOKEN service in
a language other than English.** Every statute verified below is a **written document** obligation. Several are triggered
*by* spoken conduct — the transaction was negotiated out loud in Spanish — but what the statute then demands is paper, not
an interpreter and never a phone line. The receptionist's spoken language remains the client's choice.

**Scope of this file.** California Civil Code 1632, New York DFS Part 419, and federal Title VI / EO 13166 / ADA were
researched separately and are deliberately not covered here. Everything below was read on a state legislature site, a
state agency site, or flsenate.gov. Where it was not, it says so in the heading.

---

## 1. The pattern that actually exists

Eight states have a version of the same rule, and it is worth seeing the shape before the citations:

> *If you advertise or negotiate in language X, hand the customer the contract in language X.*

The trigger is often oral. The remedy is always a document. Two of them (Arizona, Illinois) are triggered by the **oral
sales presentation** specifically, which is the closest any of this comes to regulating speech — and even there, what is
regulated is the paper that follows the speech.

| State | Citation | Trigger | What is owed | Telephone / spoken? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nevada | NRS 598.9733 | Advertises **and** negotiates (orally or in writing) in a non-English language | Full translation of the contract, every term, before signing | **No** |
| Texas | Fin. Code §341.502(a-1) | Loan terms negotiated in Spanish | Spanish *summary* in Reg Z closed-end format | **No** |
| Arizona | A.R.S. §44-1797.05(B) | Oral sales presentation in another language | Contract, receipt and all documents in that same language | **No** |
| Arizona | A.R.S. §44-1267 | Used-car warranty-waiver presentation | Waiver statement in the language of the presentation | **No** |
| Oregon | ORS 646A.124(5) | Any portion of rent-to-own transaction in another language | Required disclosures in that language | **No** |
| Oregon | ORS 86A.198 | Advertises in another language **and** substantial communication in it | GFE + TILA disclosures in English **and** that language | **No** |
| Illinois | 815 ILCS 505/2N | Retail transaction/negotiations in another language | Signed acknowledgment form, in the consumer's native language | **No** |
| Florida | §§641.305, 641.421, 636.015 | Health-plan negotiations in another language | Written translation of the contract | **No** |
| New Jersey | N.J.S.A. 17:16C-61.6(d), -100(d) | Regularly uses a non-English language in ads, forms or **face-to-face negotiations** | Two receipts — one English, one in that language | **No** |

Nevada's is the broadest and the newest, and it has **no interpreter exception** — unlike California's. Note also what
limits it: the duty attaches only if the business *advertises* in that language. A business that merely happens to have a
Spanish-speaking employee is outside it.

## 2. Texas

**Tex. Fin. Code §341.502 — "Form of loan contract and related documents"**
<https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FI/htm/FI.341.htm>

> "(a-1) If the terms of the agreement for a loan under Subsection (a) were negotiated in Spanish, a copy of a summary of
> those terms and other pertinent information shall be provided to the debtor in Spanish in a form identical to
> disclosures required for a closed-end transaction under 12 C.F.R. Section 1026.18."

Names **Spanish only**. Covers Ch. 342 loans, Ch. 348 retail installment transactions, and OCCC-regulated home equity
loans. A **summary**, not the contract — the 2005 committee substitute (H.B. 1547) removed the full-document version.
Written. Does not touch telephone or spoken service.

**Tex. Fin. Code §342.457(e)(1)** — automobile club memberships sold with a loan. The Commissioner "shall … adopt a rule
providing for disclosure in Spanish of the information required by Subsection (d)." A duty on the regulator to produce a
form; the lender's duty is to use it. Written.

**Chapter 343 (Home Loans) — no requirement found.** The suspicion that a Texas home-loan translation rule sits in Ch. 343
is **wrong**. The chapter exists (245 lines of text) and contains zero Spanish, translation or non-English-language
provisions.

**Business & Commerce Code — no contract-translation requirement found.** All 700 chapter slots were fetched and
grepped. The only non-English provisions are signage and solicitation disclosures, none of them about contracts:
§17.922 and §17.924 (clothing-donation collection disclosures "in English and Spanish"), §102.101 and §114.0053 (posted
signs), and §101.003(b) (international matchmaking organizations must give a recruit the required information "in the
recruit's native language"). §17.46(b)(28) *prohibits* translating titles like "notary" to imply legal authority — a
prohibition, not a service duty.

## 3. Arizona

**A.R.S. §44-1797.05 — "Written contract; contents"** <https://www.azleg.gov/ars/44/01797-05.htm>

> "B. No agreement of the buyer of a contract for discount buying services shall be effective unless the contract, receipt
> and all other documents pertaining to the contract are written in the same language used in the oral sales presentation.
> If a language which cannot be written is used in the oral sales presentation, such documents may be in English."

Subsections (C) and (D) extend this to the "Notice to buyer" and the cancellation form, each "in the language used in the
oral sales presentation." **Names no language** — any language. Covers discount buying services only. Written, and the
sanction is that the agreement is simply not effective. Does not touch telephone or spoken service.

**A.R.S. §44-1267** — used motor vehicles, waiver of the implied warranty of merchantability. The waiver is valid only if
the purchaser signs a conspicuous statement "that is written in the language in which the presentation was made."
Written. (Fetched from azleg.gov; a later re-fetch was rate-limited, so the quote is from the first successful retrieval
of `/ars/44/01267.htm`.)

**A.R.S. §6-1411 — "Disclosure requirements"** <https://www.azleg.gov/ars/6/01411.htm>

> "All premium finance agreements shall disclose in English and Spanish, in close proximity to the signature lines, that
> the borrower may request the Spanish language disclosure before signing any documents."

Names **Spanish**. Insurance premium finance. Written.

**A.R.S. §20-1119** <https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/01119.htm> — the inverse of a requirement. A translated insurance policy
"does not amplify, extend or modify the terms of the English language version" if it carries the prescribed disclaimer.
This is a safe harbour for insurers who translate, not a duty to translate.

**Coverage caveat, stated rather than glossed.** An exhaustive sweep of Title 44's 813 sections was attempted twice and
azleg.gov rate-limited it both times (615 of 813 failed with HTTP 429 on the first pass; the slow retry was throttled at
the index). The four sections above were each fetched and read directly. **A Title 44 provision outside them could have
been missed**, and Titles 6, 20 and 32 were never swept — only the named sections were read.

## 4. Oregon

All three verified against the current ORS on the Legislature's own site.

**ORS 646A.124(5) — lease-purchase (rent-to-own) general disclosure requirements**
<https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646A.html>

> "(5) If any portion of the transaction is conducted in any language other than English, the disclosures required under
> ORS 646A.120 to 646A.134 shall be in the language other than English. This subsection does not apply if any portion of
> the transaction is conducted through an interpreter supplied by the lessee."

**Names no language.** Lease-purchase agreements only. The trigger is the lowest of any statute found — "any portion."
The obligation is the written disclosures, which 646A.124(3) requires "clearly and conspicuously in writing … on the face
of the contract above the line for the consumer's signature." The interpreter clause is an **exemption**, not a duty to
supply one. Does not touch telephone or spoken service.

**ORS 86A.198 — "Materials in languages other than English; requirements"**
<https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors086A.html>

A mortgage banker, broker or loan originator must provide the listed materials in writing where it (a) advertises or
solicits in a language other than English, **and** (b) negotiates a residential mortgage transaction in which "a
substantial portion of the communication with the borrower … takes place in the language other than English." What is
owed, in English *and* that language: the RESPA good faith estimate, the TILA/Reg Z disclosures, and —

> "(c) A statement notifying the borrower that loan documents associated with the transaction will be in English and
> advising the borrower to obtain appropriate assistance with any necessary translations."

Subsection (3) puts the translation burden on the state: the DCBS Director "shall develop and distribute translated
versions … in the three languages other than English that are most commonly spoken in this state." Note (c) — the statute
expressly contemplates that **the contract itself stays English**. Written throughout.

**ORS 646A.090(3)(a) — motor vehicle retail installment contracts (spot delivery)**
Credit line `[Formerly 646.877; 2017 c.241 §1; 2023 c.400 §37; 2025 c.81 §1]`, so the language text is new in 2025. The
prescribed buyer disclosure form ends:

> "This disclosure is available in multiple languages upon request. Please ask the seller if you would like to receive
> this document in a language other than English."

And the Attorney General "shall … (B) Translate the form into **Spanish, Vietnamese, Chinese, Russian and Korean**." Named
languages, written form, available **on request** — the dealer's duty is to honour a request, not to anticipate it.

**ORS Chapter 83 (Retail Installment Contracts) — no requirement found.** Fetched in full; zero hits for Spanish,
translation, interpreter or non-English language.

## 5. Illinois

**815 ILCS 505/2N — "Non-English language transaction"**
<https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=2356&ChapterID=67>

This is **not** a contract-translation statute, which is the thing most likely to be misread about it. It requires signed
acknowledgment forms. Where the consumer used their own interpreter, subsection (a) requires both consumer and interpreter
to sign stating the obligations "were explained to me in my native language." Where the **retailer** acted as interpreter,
subsection (b) bites harder:

> "(b) If (i) a person conducts, in a language other than English, a retail transaction or negotiations related to a
> retail transaction resulting in a written contract and (ii) the retailer or an employee of the retailer acted as the
> consumer's interpreter … the retailer must have the consumer sign the following form in the consumer's native language"

So one document — the acknowledgment — must be in the consumer's language. The contract need not be. Subsection (c): if the
language "cannot be written," the form is in English. Subsection (e) excludes credit card transactions. **Names no
language.** Written. Source: P.A. 92-478, eff. 8-23-01.

**815 ILCS 405 (Retail Installment Sales Act) — no requirement found.**
**815 ILCS 375 (Motor Vehicle Retail Installment Sales Act) — no requirement found.**
Both acts were fetched in full from ilga.gov and grepped. Neither contains any Spanish, translation, interpreter or
non-English-language provision. The suspicion that a translation rule sits in 815 ILCS 405 or 375 is **wrong** — it is in
the Consumer Fraud Act instead.

Incidental, and not a requirement: 815 ILCS 505/2AA(b)(3) and (5) *permit* an immigration assistance service to translate
forms and documents. It is a list of what such a business may lawfully do, not a duty to serve anyone in any language.

## 6. Florida

Three parallel provisions, all verified in the 2025 statutes on flsenate.gov. All three are headed "Language used in
contracts and advertisements; translations."

| Citation | Entity | URL |
|---|---|---|
| §641.305(1)(b) | Health maintenance organizations | <https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/641.305> |
| §641.421(1)(b) | Prepaid health clinics | <https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/641.421> |
| §636.015(1)(b) | Prepaid limited health service organizations | <https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/636.015> |

The HMO version, §641.305(1)(b):

> "If the negotiations by a health maintenance organization with a member leading up to the effectuation of a health
> maintenance contract are conducted in a language other than English, the health maintenance organization shall supply
> to the member a written translation of the contract, which translation accurately reflects the substance of the
> contract and is in the language used to negotiate the contract."

Each begins "All … contracts or forms shall be printed in English." §641.421 adds a required caption, in the language of
the translation: **"READ THIS FIRST / This is a translation of the document that you are about to sign."** Subsection (2)
of each requires non-English **advertisements** — expressly including "any radio or television transmittal" — to also be
available in English and furnished to the office on request. That is the inverse of a service duty: it makes the
advertiser answerable in English, not the business answerable in Spanish.

**Names no language.** Written. **No general Florida consumer-contract translation requirement was found** — these three
are confined to health plans.

## 7. New Jersey — the premise was wrong, but a real rule exists elsewhere

**There is no contract-translation provision in N.J.S.A. 56:12.** The Plain Language Act (56:12-1 to -13) requires plain
**English**, not translation. §56:12-12 is "Injunctions; attorney fees, court costs." The translation law that is widely
believed to be there is a **bill that has never been enacted** — S890 / A1558 (2022-2023), whose statement reads "This
bill requires certain consumer contracts to include translations to languages other than English," and which died in
committee. *UNVERIFIED — secondary source:* LegiScan shows both as dead, and a 2026 reintroduction (S4589) surfaced in
search results but its status was not checked. Primary source needed: pub.njleg.gov bill status for S4589 (2026).

The entire New Jersey statutes were searched on the Legislature's own Folio system
(<https://lis.njleg.state.nj.us/nxt/gateway.dll/statutes/1>, "updated through P.L.2026, c.70") for *Spanish* (128 hits),
*language other than English* (18 hits) and *interpreter* (27 hits). The real rule is in **Title 17**:

**N.J.S.A. 17:16C-61.6(d) — Retail Installment Sales Act, receipt**

> "(d) A retail seller who in the ordinary course of business regularly uses a language other than English in any
> advertising or other solicitation of retail buyers, or in any printed forms for use by retail buyers, or in any
> face-to-face negotiations with retail buyers shall deliver the two copies of the receipt to a retail buyer whose
> principal language is such other language one in English and one in the other language."

**N.J.S.A. 17:16C-100(d)** — identical, for **home repair contractors**. Both from L.1968, amended L.1975.

Note the trigger: "regularly uses … in the ordinary course of business," reached by advertising, printed forms **or
face-to-face negotiations**. Note also the limit — what must be bilingual is the *receipt*, which carries the three-day
rescission notice, not the contract. **Names no language.** Written. Neither touches telephone or spoken service.

Four more New Jersey written duties on private businesses:

- **N.J.S.A. 56:12-34** — Lemon Law. At purchase or lease, the manufacturer or lessor must provide the consumer a written
  statement of rights "printed in both the English and **Spanish** languages." Written.
- **N.J.S.A. 56:11-34** — a consumer reporting agency "that compiles and maintains files on consumers on a nationwide
  basis" must make the file disclosure available "upon the consumer's request in **Spanish** or any other language that
  the Director … determines is the first language of a significant number of consumers," and "in at least the 10 languages
  other than English and Spanish that are most frequently spoken." The statute does not specify the channel; it is a
  disclosure duty, not an interpreter duty.
- **N.J.S.A. 48:3-85(a)(1)(a)** — electric power and gas suppliers must "provide a one-page information sheet in a
  12-point font summarizing the material terms and conditions of the contract in English and **Spanish**." Written.
- **N.J.S.A. 56:8-176(e)** — prepaid calling cards; see §9 below, because it is the one near-miss on the telephone question.

**Note: New Jersey's official statute site is a session-based Folio NXT system with no stable per-section URLs.** The
entry point above is citable; individual sections are reached through its search. Each quote here was read from that
system.

## 8. Washington, Massachusetts, Nevada, New Mexico

### Washington — no contract-translation requirement found

809 chapters across 23 titles (19, 20, 21, 22, 30A, 30B, 31, 32, 33, 46, 48, 49, 58, 59, 61, 62A, 63, 64, 70, 7, 9, 9A,
18) were fetched in full from `app.leg.wa.gov/RCW` and grepped. **Nothing requires a private business to translate a
consumer contract negotiated in another language.** What exists is foreclosure and association notices:

- **RCW 64.32.200** (horizontal property regimes), **RCW 64.34.360 / 64.34.364** (condominiums), **RCW 64.90.455 /
  64.90.640** (common interest communities) — association preforeclosure notices must "(ii) Be provided in English and
  any other language indicated as a preference for correspondence by a unit owner. Translation inaccuracies shall not
  diminish a good faith effort to provide notice in a preferred language other than English."
  <https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=64.32.200> — this is the most owner-language-responsive duty found in any
  state, and it is still a written notice.
- **RCW 61.12.040(3)** — judicial foreclosure: "The written notice must be in English and Spanish."
- **RCW 61.24** — the Department of Commerce "must develop the model language in both English and Spanish and both
  versions must be contained in the same letter."
- **RCW 32.32** — on a savings bank conversion, if a significant number of account holders speak another language and a
  newspaper in that language is published in the area, "an appropriate translation of the notice shall also be published."

*Scope caveat:* 23 of Washington's 101 titles were swept, chosen for consumer, financial, insurance, real-property and
commercial subject matter. The remaining titles were not.

### Massachusetts — no contract-translation requirement found, but the one spoken duty in this whole file is here

The full General Laws were searched on malegislature.gov for *Spanish* (22 hits) and *language other than English*
(26 hits).

**M.G.L. c.93 §40A** <https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93/Section40A> — dishonoured checks:

> "The written demand for payment required by this section shall be in the following form and shall be printed in at
> least ten point type in both the English and Spanish languages"

A private payee's duty, **Spanish** named, written, no telephone.

**M.G.L. c.111 §25J — "Competent interpreter services in acute-care hospitals."** This is the **one obligation found
anywhere in this research that is spoken, and it expressly reaches the telephone**:

> "(b) Every acute-care hospital … shall provide competent interpreter services in connection with all emergency room
> services provided to every non-English speaker … each such hospital shall use reasonable judgment as to whether to
> employ, or to contract for the on-call use of one or more interpreters for particular languages when needed, or to use
> competent **telephonic or televiewing** interpreter services."

Telephonic interpreting is permitted only where there is "no reasonable way to anticipate the need" or an employed
interpreter is unavailable. Enforced through licensing plus a private right of action with a $250-per-violation floor.
**M.G.L. c.123 §23A** does the same for hospitals providing acute psychiatric services.

*Corrected on review: this section said "these bind private hospitals". That is not established here.* The statute binds
acute-care hospitals "as defined in section 25B", and **§25B was not read**, so whether the duty reaches private hospitals
as well as public ones is unsettled in this research. Two other things are worth stating precisely, because the paragraph
above reads stronger than the statute is: telephonic interpreting is **not** the mandated method but a *restricted
fallback* — permitted "only" where there is no reasonable way to anticipate the need for a language, or an inability to
provide an interpreter in a given instance. Either way a hospital emergency room is a long way from a small business's
phone line, and the duty is the hospital's own rather than a vendor's.

Also: **c.222 §21** — a non-attorney notary advertising "in a language other than English" must include, "prominently
displayed in the same language," the statement "I am not an attorney and I have no authority to give advice on
immigration or other legal matters." A disclaimer duty attached to the advertiser's own language choice.
**c.176O §6(a)(10)** — an insurance carrier's evidence of coverage must contain "a statement detailing what translator and
interpretation services are available to assist insureds." A duty to *describe* what is available, not to provide it.

### Nevada — the broadest statute found anywhere in scope

**NRS 598.9733** <https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-598.html> (added 2021; the chapter page has no per-section anchors):

> "1. A person who, in the course of his or her business or occupation, advertises in a language other than English and
> negotiates orally or in writing any of the transactions listed in subsection 3 in a language other than English … shall
> deliver a translation of the contract or agreement that results from such advertising and negotiations in the language
> that was used in the advertisement and negotiation of the contract or agreement to the person who is a party to the
> contract or agreement…"

Subsection 2: the translation must be delivered **before execution** and "include, without limitation, every term and
condition in the contract or agreement." Subsection 3 lists the covered transactions: (a) loans or credit secured by
non-real property for personal, family or household purposes; (b) residential leases, subleases or rental agreements with
a term of at least one month; (c) unsecured personal-use loans. Subsection 4 exempts a bank, savings and loan, savings
bank, thrift or credit union that has a physical location — except for credit card issuance and automobile loans.

**Names no language.** Written. **No interpreter exception** — unlike California's statute, nothing lets the business off
because the customer brought their own translator. **NRS 598.9737** lists what may stay in English (names, addresses,
brand and trade names, trademarks, model designations, alphanumeric codes, numerals, dollar amounts, dates, and
"individual words or expressions that do not have a generally accepted non-English translation"). **NRS 598.9739**: "If a
person fails to comply … the aggrieved party may rescind the contract or agreement." Does not touch telephone or spoken
service.

Two narrower Nevada rules, both a right to **view** rather than receive:

- **NRS 482.3277** — a vehicle dealer "who advertises that the Spanish language is spoken at his or her place of business
  or who conducts business by communicating in Spanish" must, on request, "allow the purchaser or prospective purchaser to
  view the version of the forms for the application for credit and contracts … which have been translated into Spanish."
- **NRS 490.380** — the same for off-highway vehicle dealers.
- **NRS 97.299(3)** — "The Commissioner shall arrange for or otherwise cause the translation into Spanish of the forms
  prescribed pursuant to subsection 1." A duty on the regulator, which is what makes the dealer duties workable.

### New Mexico — *UNVERIFIED — secondary source*

Reported as unverified, from secondary sources only:

- **NMSA 58-15-17** — a small loan licensee must deliver a disclosure statement "in English or Spanish"; a 2017 amendment

- **NMSA 58-15-32** — payday lenders were said to provide the agreement in English, Spanish or another language set by the

- **NMSA 58-15-38** — a notice posted at each licensed place of business in both English and Spanish.

## 9. The telephone question: no requirement found

**No state or federal law was found requiring any particular language for a recorded telephone greeting, voicemail,
auto-attendant or IVR menu at a private business.** This is a clean negative and it is the finding that matters most for
our own build. Searched for directly, several ways, including vendor and firm sources that were not relied on.

What the searching turned up instead, and why none of it is the rule:

- **New York bills (A10668, A10142, 2009; A5194, 2013)** would have required Spanish telephone customer service — on
  **state agencies and public authorities**, and on contracts over $1,000,000 awarded by the state. *UNVERIFIED —
  secondary source* as to enactment; they appear to be bills, not law. They do not reach private businesses regardless.
- **California's chatbot law** governs customer-service platforms but concerns **reaching a human**, not language.
  (Out of scope here; the California researcher has it.)
- **Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006** — the closest federal analogue, and it is **permissive**. §1006.34(d)(3)(vi)
  *allows* a debt collector to include a Spanish statement offering a Spanish validation notice; having offered it, it
  must then honour a request. The Bureau "declines to make the Spanish-language disclosures … mandatory." §1006.18(e)(4)
  requires the disclosure in Spanish where the collector's own first communication was in Spanish — the same
  you-started-it logic as the state contract statutes, and still about a notice.
- **N.J.S.A. 56:8-176 — the one genuine near-miss, and worth reading closely.** Subsection (f) *requires* a prepaid
  calling service provider to run a 24-hour customer service telephone number, and expressly contemplates that "customer
  service may be provided by a combination of a live operator, interactive voice response, and electronic voice recording
  … but live operator service shall be available 24 hours a day, seven days a week." Subsection (e) then says:

  > "If a language other than English is predominantly used on a prepaid calling card, its packaging, or in point of sale
  > advertising or promotion … then the disclosures required by this section shall be disclosed in that language on that
  > card, packaging, advertisement or promotion."

  So a statute that mandates a phone line *and* a non-English disclosure duty exists — but **(e)'s enumerated media are
  the card, the packaging, the advertisement and the promotion. The phone line is not among them.** Subsection (b) does
  let a provider satisfy certain disclosures "through the customer service number," which is the seam where an argument
  could be made. No case law was checked. This is the closest thing found to a non-English telephone obligation on a
  private business in the United States, and it still is not one.
