# Connecting crew locations to dispatch without hosting trackers

The cleanest way for the "Crew location tools" step to work is a vendor-run OAuth connection to a field-service or fleet tool the owner already pays for, with the receptionist reading a position only at the moment a caller asks, keeping nothing, and reporting how old the position is. **ServiceM8 is the only tool found where staff GPS (`staff_locations`) and job assignment (`manage_jobs`, `manage_schedule`) sit under one owner-approved OAuth consent**, and the location scope is flagged as requiring user consent ([ServiceM8 authentication](https://developer.servicem8.com/docs/authentication)). Motive and Samsara are the next best fits but report vehicles, not people. The open-source trackers are real and maintained, but none matches "a vendor runs it and the owner signs in": Traccar, the only fleet-grade one, is a server someone must host, and it authenticates with a pasted token or Basic credentials rather than a sign-in screen. Zero data retention is easy to honor if we persist nothing, but no source found says that not storing a location removes the consent and notice duty, because the Florida statute read attaches that duty to using a tracking app to find a person, which a live lookup still is. The consent rules are state-by-state and mostly unverified at statute level, so the step must make the owner, not us, the party who states what crew were told. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Only a few commercial tools expose where a person is

Of the tools surveyed, documented location APIs exist for ServiceM8 (staff GPS), Samsara (vehicle GPS in the `gps` stat of `GET /fleet/vehicles/stats/feed`, needing the Read Vehicle Statistics scope), Geotab (`DeviceStatusInfo` carries current latitude and longitude), Motive (a Locations permission in its developer portal) and Verizon Connect Reveal (a Vehicle Update API that polls GPS one vehicle per call) ([Samsara](https://developers.samsara.com/reference/getvehiclestatsfeed), [Geotab](https://developers.geotab.com/myGeotab/apiReference/objects/DeviceStatusInfo), [Motive](https://developer-docs.gomotive.com/docs/oauth-20), [Verizon Connect](https://fim.eu.fleetmatics.com/content/api/20601b05-c704-4bca-8c60-876c4656d76d.fleetmatics-p-eu/documents/vehicle_update_api.htm)). For Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz and ServiceTitan, no documented technician-location endpoint was found; several vendor pages blocked the fetcher, so that is "not confirmed either way", not "absent". Google Maps location sharing and Life360 have no official public API, so neither can be a vendor-run connection ([Google Latitude thread](https://groups.google.com/g/google-latitude-api/c/58NUsFkNO34)). The distinction that matters for a small crew is person versus vehicle: telematics tells the receptionist where the van is, which is only a proxy for the technician.

Sign-in method decides which of these fits the rule that the owner signs in rather than pasting a secret. ServiceM8 public apps use OAuth 2.0 with a consent screen, 3600-second tokens with refresh, and owner revocation by disabling the add-on, though the developer must register as a Development Partner. Motive uses OAuth where the fleet customer clicks Install, with a developer account (one source says it must be set up with Motive support, which conflicts with "sign up yourself") ([Motive OAuth](https://developer-docs.gomotive.com/docs/oauth-20)). Jobber is OAuth-only, and a marketplace listing needs a tester account and review ([Jobber docs](https://developer.getjobber.com/docs/getting_started)). Samsara tokens are created by the customer in the dashboard, with OAuth recommended for marketplace apps ([Samsara authentication](https://developers.samsara.com/docs/authentication)). Geotab's guidance is that the customer creates a service account and shares its credentials with the integrator ([Geotab](https://support.geotab.com/software-integration/doc/service-acct-api)), and Housecall Pro offers an API key on its MAX plan only, whose key reaches all account data; Housecall Pro says it will not support custom integrations built on it ([Housecall Pro](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6790722-how-to-make-an-api-key)). Handing us a key or password is the pattern the product rules rule out, so Geotab and Housecall Pro fail the sign-in test even where the data exists.

Dispatch writes follow the same split. ServiceM8 has `create_jobs` (which may incur account charges), `manage_jobs` and `manage_schedule`; Samsara creates routes with `POST /fleet/routes` at 100 requests per minute; Motive lists a Dispatches permission; ServiceTitan exposes dispatch endpoints, though a third-party spec of assignment operations appears mislabeled ([Samsara routes](https://developers.samsara.com/reference/createroute), [Fivetran](https://support.fivetran.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/42367186233751-ServiceTitan-Dispatch-API)). Whether location lookup alone, or also assigning jobs, is within the receptionist's remit is a product decision for the owner of the product; the sources show only what each tool permits.

## Open-source trackers work, but someone must host them

All three trackers are licensed and current. Traccar's server is Apache 2.0, Java, version 6.17.0 released 2026-11-01, with a phone client at 10.2.4 on Google Play, the App Store and as a GitHub APK ([Traccar GitHub](https://github.com/traccar/traccar), [Traccar Client](https://www.traccar.org/client/)). OwnTracks Recorder (GPL v2 or later, C, plain files, release 1.0.7) receives from the OwnTracks apps over MQTT or HTTP; the Android app is on Google Play and F-Droid, and the iOS app (release 27.1.2) is a separate repo whose licence and App Store listing I could not confirm ([Recorder](https://github.com/owntracks/recorder), [OwnTracks Android](https://github.com/owntracks/android)). Dawarich (AGPL-3.0, release 1.23.0) is a personal location-history app, described as a self-hostable alternative to Google Timeline, not fleet software ([Dawarich](https://github.com/Freika/dawarich)). Nextcloud PhoneTrack, Overland and GPSLogger were not researched.

The reading side favors Traccar. Its REST API returns last-known positions from `GET /positions`, or history when a device and ISO 8601 range are given, and `GET /devices` lists devices; standard users see only their own ([Traccar openapi](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/traccar/traccar/master/openapi.yaml)). Auth is a session cookie, HTTP Basic, or a bearer token, and the live WebSocket accepts session cookies only ([Traccar API](https://www.traccar.org/traccar-api/)). Recorder's `/api/0/last` and `/locations` documentation describes no authentication, so protection is whatever reverse proxy the host adds ([Recorder API](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/owntracks/recorder/master/API.md)). I infer, untested, that we could read Traccar with an owner-pasted URL and token; I found no statement on whether a token can be read-only or how it expires, and no rate limits.

Hosting is the obstacle for a small shop. Traccar's own guide puts self-hosting at roughly $5 to $20 per month for a VPS that the business installs and upgrades; managed hosting starts at **$9.95 per month** for a single-user shared account and **$49.95** for a dedicated server, but the pricing page does not say whether API access is included on the cheaper tier ([Managed vs self-hosted](https://www.traccar.org/managed-vs-self-hosted/), [Pricing](https://www.traccar.org/pricing/)). Dawarich Cloud runs EUR 59.99 to 119.99 per year with API request caps ([Dawarich Cloud](https://dawarich.app/cloud/)); no hosted OwnTracks was found, though absence is unproven. Managed Traccar is therefore the one open-source route that approximates "a vendor runs it", while still using a pasted token rather than a sign-in. The record to weigh against the pattern we already use is that every owner would need their own server or account, which our service reads but cannot operate for them under the connectors-only rule.

The phone side carries operational risk the receptionist must absorb. On iOS, Traccar Client needs "always" location and background refresh, and swiping the app away makes iOS terminate it so it cannot report; on Android it needs battery-optimisation exemption and vendor battery savers may kill it ([Traccar troubleshooting](https://www.traccar.org/client-troubleshooting/)). Stop detection pauses updates while stationary, and OwnTracks' iOS Manual and Quiet modes do not track in the background ([OwnTracks modes](https://owntracks.org/booklet/features/location/)). The practical result is that a stale or missing position is routine, so location age is part of the answer, not a footnote.

## Not storing location is the easy part of zero data retention

Retention is where the tools differ most from the product's stance. Traccar keeps all history by default and documents no built-in retention setting, offering `DELETE /positions` and a documented daily cron script (shown at one year) instead; OwnTracks Recorder can run with `--norec` so only the last position is kept ([Traccar clear history](https://www.traccar.org/clear-history/), [Recorder](https://github.com/owntracks/recorder)). Among commercial products, Hubstaff keeps location for one month on its listed plans, and QuickBooks Time does not collect points on break or when clocked out ([Hubstaff](https://support.hubstaff.com/data-retention-comparison/), [QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/time-tracking/resources/gps-tracking-faq/)). Jobber records location at clock-in, clock-out and visit completion plus live tracking; most platforms set no default limit, so history accumulates unless an admin sets one. No product examined was found to offer a read-on-request, store-nothing mode, which is what our side would do: call the vendor, answer the caller, persist neither coordinates nor the lookup's content.

That design reduces what could leak or be kept indefinitely, which matters because FTC consumer location cases required published retention schedules and one complaint treated indefinite retention as unfair; those were data-broker cases, not employer cases ([FTC Kochava](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data)). **But no source found says non-retention removes notice or consent duties.** Florida's statute, read via a fetch summary, forbids using a tracking app to find a person's location without consent, and Apple requires consent to collect location even if the data is anonymous ([Fla. Stat. 934.425](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/934.425), [Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The vendor still retains whatever it collects under its own rules, so the receptionist's zero retention covers only our side. Call content stays unretained either way, but a location lookup during a call is a new fact the owner must decide how to describe.

## Consent rules are patchwork, and Florida's exception is unverified

No federal statute specific to employer GPS tracking was found; commentary says plaintiffs rely on state law and privacy torts ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/resourcesandtools/hr-topics/risk-management/pages/monitoring-employees-gps.aspx)). Florida defines "person" to exclude a business entity and, per the fetch summary, excepts a person acting in good faith for a business entity's legitimate business purpose; violation is a third-degree felony, and the 2024 amendment extended the ban from installing to using. The exact wording of that exception, and any case law on employee phones, were not verified ([Fla. Stat. 934.425](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/934.425)). Connecticut, New York and Delaware have written-notice regimes (New York and Delaware also require acknowledgment), but as found they target premises, phone, email or internet monitoring, and whether they reach phone location is not established ([Conn. 31-48d](https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/2022/title-31/chapter-557/section-31-48d/), [NY CVR 52-c](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVR/52-C*2), [Delaware](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title19/c007/sc01/index.html)). California Penal Code 637.7 is the most location-specific rule found, with a consent exception for vehicle owners that commentary says has no phone equivalent ([CalChamber](https://p-hrcalifornia.calchamber.com/hr-library/qa/track-employees-GPS-device-cell-phone-vehicle)). Illinois, Texas, Washington and others, plus ECPA, FLSA and NLRA questions, were not researched.

What crew must be told is therefore a matter of commentary, not verified statute: write a policy covering what is tracked, when, who sees it and how long it is kept, get written acknowledgment, and track only during work hours, which that source says needs a deliberate configuration step ([fieldservicesoftware.io](https://fieldservicesoftware.io/blog/field-technician-tracking-gps-privacy-trust/)). Platform rules apply to whichever app runs on the crew phones. Google requires background location to be core functionality, with a prominent in-app disclosure, a declaration and a short video; Apple requires consent, specific purpose strings, minimisation and a privacy policy stating retention and deletion, and developer-forum reports describe an app used solely to track employees being rejected ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150?hl=en)). Because the vendor's app, not ours, runs on the phone, the vendor carries that review; our exposure is in what we display and say.

Two privacy behaviors affect answers. Samsara's Privacy Button stops real-time GPS and shows the last location, with managers not alerted per its datasheet, and Geotab has a personal-use privacy mode ([Samsara datasheet](https://www.samsara.com/pdf/docs/privacy-button-data-sheet.pdf)). No API-exposed privacy flag was found, so a private-mode vehicle looks like a stale position, and the receptionist cannot tell "off duty" from "phone dead".

## Conclusion

The tension is that the product's rules fit commercial OAuth connectors better than open-source trackers: the trackers are good software but turn the owner into a server administrator or a token-pasting integrator, while a ServiceM8 or Motive connection reuses the pattern already built for Drive. The less obvious finding is that zero data retention and consent are separate questions: not persisting coordinates is cheap and defensible, yet it does not discharge the duty to tell crew they are being located, and that duty is the owner's to meet.

The largest unknowns are factual, not design ones: whether the tools whose docs were blocked (Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz, ServiceTitan) expose technician location, the verbatim Florida business-entity exception, and whether a vendor consent flag can be read. Until those are checked, the plan should treat any claim about what is legal as "not checked".

## What this means for the signup screens and the plan

1. **Choose the source of crew location.** A screen listing the vendor-run tools with a documented location API (ServiceM8, Motive, Samsara, Verizon Connect Reveal, and Traccar managed), plus a "we do not use a location tool" answer. Tied to: only a few tools expose location; Google sharing and Life360 have no official API. Whether Geotab and Housecall Pro (key or service-account password) are offered at all is a decision for Jeff, since they conflict with "the owner signs in".
2. **Connect-then-test, as with Drive.** The owner signs in on the vendor's page, approves scopes, and the step proves the connection by reading one position and its age before moving on. Tied to: OAuth fits ServiceM8, Motive and Jobber. Failure states (consent refused, token revoked, vendor down, no staff with location) each need a fixed message.
3. **Decide the status of Traccar and OwnTracks.** Open decision for Jeff: offer Traccar managed (owner pastes URL and token, which departs from "owner signs in"), self-hosted options, or neither. Tied to: hosting burden, no read-only token scoping found, Recorder's lack of its own auth. Dawarich is personal-use software and not a fit as found.
4. **Person versus vehicle.** The owner states whether the connected source is a person's phone or a vehicle, because the receptionist's wording differs. Tied to: telematics report vehicles; ServiceM8 reports staff.
5. **Position age is part of every answer.** A state for "no position", "stale position" and "position within X minutes", with the threshold set by the owner. Tied to: iOS force-quit, battery savers, stationary pause, privacy modes appearing as stale.
6. **Owner chooses how the receptionist uses a location.** Setup questions with answer choices, shown to Jeff first, for: use location to answer "can someone get there today"; suggest a technician; or assign a job. Whether the receptionist writes to dispatch at all is a product decision; ServiceM8, Samsara and Motive permit it, others unconfirmed. Tied to: dispatch API findings.
7. **Location read on request, nothing stored on our side.** State this on the step in plain words, and say what the vendor keeps is the vendor's. Tied to: ZDR, and the finding that no commercial tool offers store-nothing. Open decision: whether to display any vendor retention setting (for example a Traccar cleanup schedule) as a note or a question to the owner.
8. **Owner confirms crew notice and consent.** A required acknowledgment, wording to be approved by Jeff, that the owner has told crew they can be located and meets their state's rules; our side does not collect crew consent. Open decision: whether to collect the owner's state and show state-specific notes (Florida, California, Connecticut, New York, Delaware were researched; most others were not). Tied to: patchwork law, unverified Florida exception, no source that non-retention lifts the duty.
9. **Work hours only.** A question asking whether lookups are limited to the owner's working hours, with the answer left to the owner. Tied to: the commentary norm and clock-in-only vendor behavior; no statute found.
10. **Disconnect and revocation.** A way to disconnect from the step and a message explaining the owner must also revoke in the vendor's settings (ServiceM8 by disabling the add-on). Tied to: owner revocation in OAuth flows.
11. **Vendor access terms before committing to any tool.** Open item: ServiceM8 Development Partner registration, Motive developer account and API terms, Samsara partner requirements, ServiceTitan and Geotab marketplace terms, Housecall Pro MAX-plan requirement, and any per-job charge on ServiceM8's `create_jobs`. Tied to: partnership-gated findings.
12. **Research still to do before building.** Whether Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz and ServiceTitan expose technician GPS; verbatim Florida 934.425(4)(g) and case law; Samsara locations-snapshot scopes; whether any vendor exposes a privacy-mode flag; other states. Each is flagged "not checked", not assumed.
